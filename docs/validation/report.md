## V1. Georeferencing vs Gazebo's GPS model

Across the full 3000 m terrain, world coordinates differ from Gazebo's NavSat conversion by at most **0.1 cm** (mean 0.1 cm). The earlier UTM grid would have differed by up to 12.20 m (grid convergence). Earth curvature makes Gazebo report altitude 35 cm higher at the terrain corners than the flat heightmap; negligible.

## V2. Rendered heightmaps vs source DEM

Reconstructed from the PNGs Gazebo loads: visual (2049x2049): RMSE 0.30 cm, max 0.53 cm; collision (1025x1025): RMSE 0.30 cm, max 0.53 cm. 16-bit quantisation step is 0.53 cm. Visual vs collision surfaces at shared samples: max 29.09 cm, so the rover (physics) and what you see (rendering) agree. These comparisons use the pre-carving heightmaps. Road carving then deliberately modifies 25.40 ha along 18.9 km of OSM roads (max cut 7.2 m, max fill 4.4 m) to represent road cuts the 30 m DEM cannot resolve.

## Known limitations

- Elevation source is 30 m; finer grids interpolate, they do not add detail. Road cuts narrower than ~30 m are not in the DEM.
- Copernicus is a surface model: canopy and structures are included in the terrain height.
- Vertical datum is EGM2008 (orthometric); a real GNSS receiver reports ellipsoidal height, which differs by the local geoid undulation.
- Imagery (Esri World Imagery) has an unknown capture date.
- Turbine model (Suzlon S52 class) is inferred from spacing and tower type.
- Foliage is decorative, placed from an RGB vegetation index, visual only.
